ReadyList Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 October 2026

In short

ReadyList is an app for shopping lists and to-do lists. Your lists are kept on your own device and the app works without an internet connection. ReadyList has no accounts, no advertising and no analytics, and it does not automatically send your lists or personal information to the developer.

Your lists leave your device only when you save, share or export them yourself, or when Android's own backup includes them, if you have turned that on. Both are explained below.

What ReadyList stores, and where

ReadyList stores what you enter in it: your lists, templates and archived lists, their items (with quantity, priority and whether they are ticked), your categories, and your theme setting.

All of this is stored on your device, in ReadyList's own storage, and everything works offline. ReadyList does not ask for or read your name, email address, phone number, location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone.

What the developer receives

ReadyList has no server, and no sync or online service of its own. The app does not automatically send your lists, your settings, personal information, or information about how you use the app to the developer or to anyone else. There is no sign-in, so the app does not tell the developer who uses it.

If you email ReadyList Support at readylist.support@gmail.com, the developer receives your email address and the information you choose to include, such as a description of a problem or a file you attach. That information is used to respond to and handle your inquiry.

Support emails are kept for as long as reasonably necessary to respond to inquiries and provide assistance, or to meet applicable legal obligations. You can request deletion of your support correspondence by emailing readylist.support@gmail.com.

Google Play gives app developers general statistics, such as the number of installs and crash reports that you have allowed your device to send to Google. These come from Google Play and your device's settings, not from ReadyList, and are covered by Google's privacy policy.

Backups you make yourself

In Settings, Save backup to device writes one file with all your lists to a place you choose, and Share backup sends that file to an app you pick. Restore from backup reads a backup file you choose, shows what it contains, and replaces the lists on this device only if you confirm.

Backup files are ordinary files and are not password-protected. Once saved or shared, who can read them depends on where you put them or who you sent them to.

Sharing lists and bringing items in

Share list sends one list as a CSV (spreadsheet) file to the app you pick, and Import list reads such a file back. You can also send ReadyList items from another app: a ReadyList transfer file or text shared or opened with ReadyList, a readylist:// link, or Paste from ChatGPT, which reads your clipboard once, only when you tap it.

All of these happen only when you start them. ReadyList checks what it receives and shows it to you before changing anything. ReadyList does not connect to ChatGPT or any other service: you copy and paste between apps yourself, and what you enter in another app is covered by that app's privacy policy.

Android's own backup

Android has a backup feature of its own, run by Android and Google and controlled in your phone's settings. ReadyList does not run it and cannot see it. If you have turned on backup on your device:

  • Android may include your ReadyList lists in your device's backup to your Google account. ReadyList allows this only when Android reports that the backup is encrypted on your device before it is uploaded.
  • Android may copy your lists directly to a new device when you set one up.
  • On Android 7 and 8, ReadyList does not take part in Android's backup.

Only your lists are included. The developer has no access to these backups; they are handled under Google's privacy policy, and you can turn them off in your phone's backup settings. Android's backup is not guaranteed to run or to restore, so if your lists matter to you, use Save backup to device as well.

Internet access

The Android app includes the standard internet permission that comes with the framework ReadyList is built on. ReadyList does not use the internet to send or receive your lists.

Advertising, analytics and tracking

ReadyList contains no advertising, analytics or tracking tools, and does not sell or share your data with advertisers or anyone else.

Deleting your data

Your data stays on your device until you delete it. The developer holds no copy of your lists or other app data, so there is nothing in the app for the developer to delete. Emails you send to ReadyList Support are separate: they are not part of the app (see "What the developer receives").

  • Delete lists, items or categories inside the app.
  • To delete everything, uninstall ReadyList, or clear its storage in Android's settings (Apps > ReadyList > Storage).
  • Backup and list files you saved or shared are separate files: delete them where you saved them.
  • If Android's backup holds a copy, you can manage it in your Google account's backup settings. With backup on, Android may bring your lists back when you reinstall ReadyList.
  • To have your support correspondence deleted, email readylist.support@gmail.com.

Security

Your lists are kept in ReadyList's private storage on your device, which other apps cannot normally read. Their safety also depends on your device, so use a screen lock and keep your phone up to date. Files you save or share are not encrypted by ReadyList.

Children

ReadyList is a general-purpose app. It is not directed at children, and the app does not collect information from anyone.

Changes to this policy

If ReadyList changes in a way that affects your privacy, this policy will be updated, with a new date, before that change reaches the app.
